5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding Tool

When panic pulls your mind into future catastrophes, grounding anchors you back into physical reality.

0 of 5 anchored
5 Things you can SEE

Look around: shadow, book spine, coffee mug, tree branch, pen.

4 Things you can TOUCH

Feel the texture of your jeans, desk edge, shoes on floor, hair.

3 Things you can HEAR

Computer hum, distant traffic, footsteps, clock ticking.

2 Things you can SMELL

Fresh air, coffee, sweater, shampoo, or hand soap.

1 Thing you can TASTE

A sip of cold water, mint, tea, or aftertaste of lunch.

Acute Panic Attack Protocol

When heart rate surges and hyperventilation begins:

  • Change Temperature: Splash cold water on your face or hold an ice cube (triggers the mammalian dive reflex).
  • Prolong the Exhale: Breathe out twice as long as you breathe in (e.g., inhale 3s, exhale 6s).
  • Name Reality: Say out loud: "My nervous system is having a false alarm. I am safe in this room."
⏱️

The 15-Minute Study Rule

For overwhelming paralysis when facing huge papers or exams:

  • Lower the bar to an absurd degree: agree to sit down for just 15 minutes.
  • Write one single messy paragraph or read 2 pages.
  • When the alarm goes off, you have full psychological permission to stop. 80% of the time, overcoming the initiation barrier is all you needed.
🌙

Student Insomnia Protocol

Stopping late-night rumination:

  • The 20-Minute Bed Rule: If awake for more than 20 minutes, get out of bed. Sit in dim light and read a boring book until drowsy.
  • Bedside Worry Pad: Write down your to-do list so your subconscious stops cycling through them.
  • Turn on our ambient sound generator in the navbar.

How to Use Campus Counseling

Student health resources you've already paid for:

  • Most universities provide 6–12 free counseling sessions per academic year covered by your student activity fees.
  • Same-day drop-in triage hours exist for acute distress.
  • Group therapy for social anxiety, grief, and graduate student burnout are widely available.
